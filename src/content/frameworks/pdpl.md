## What the PDPL is

The Personal Data Protection Law is the UAE's first standalone federal law
dedicated to personal data. It establishes a general framework: organisations
need a valid reason to process personal data, must be transparent about what
they do with it, must keep it secure, and must respect a set of individual
rights.

## Who it applies to

- Organisations established in the UAE that process personal data, wherever the
  processing physically happens.
- Organisations established outside the UAE that process the personal data of
  individuals located in the UAE.

Government entities and the financial free zones (DIFC, ADGM) sit outside the
federal regime and are covered by their own rules.

## Key obligations

- Identify a lawful basis for each processing activity.
- Give clear notice to individuals about what you collect and why.
- Honour data subject rights: access, correction, deletion, restriction,
  portability, and objection.
- Keep records of processing activities.
- Apply appropriate technical and organisational security measures.
- Assess and, where required, report personal data breaches.
- Meet conditions before transferring personal data outside the UAE.
- Appoint a Data Protection Officer where the processing is high-risk.

## Penalties and consequences

Administrative penalties are defined by Cabinet decision. Beyond fines, the
regulator can order an organisation to stop a processing activity. Sector
regulators (healthcare, finance, telecoms) can impose their own consequences,
and non-compliance frequently surfaces in customer due-diligence and contracts.
